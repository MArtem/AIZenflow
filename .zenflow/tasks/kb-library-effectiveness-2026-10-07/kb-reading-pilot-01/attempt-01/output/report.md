Publication invariants: cancellation is cooperative; a producer may ignore it and return a value or generic error later. Cancellation alone cannot prove suppression or undo an already committed side effect. Only the still-authorized operation may publish success, generic failure, loading/reset state or related observer effects. Check cancellation and operation identity/current generation at the publication boundary for both success and generic failure. The check and mutation must follow the same isolation/ordering contract; suspension between a successful check and publication can invalidate the decision. Actor isolation alone does not eliminate reentrancy after await. Request identity alone does not prove cancellation safety. A current operation's generic failure must follow the actual product error contract; obsolete failure must not overwrite its replacement. CancellationError should propagate without automatic user-visible failure unless the product explicitly requires that behavior.

Lifetime/ordering invariants: identify creator, retainer, cancellation and await/result owner; start/replacement/duplicate-start policies; replacement, dismissal, logout or session invalidation timing; release and child propagation. Structured children are bounded by parent lifetime. Unstructured work needs an explicit owner and cancellation policy; detached work cannot evade actor isolation or silently outlive its operation. Weak capture followed by a strong local before await can retain the owner; deinit-only cancellation may never occur when the task retains it. Replacement/invalidation must establish the new authority before older completions can mutate shared state. Older requests need not finish first. Match any identity, serialization, merging, retry or idempotency rule to actual consumer semantics and committed effects. Keep UI state on its actual actor and heavy computation off UI executor; async is not proof of background execution. Work, cleanup and cancellation latency require bounds.

## Q2 — synchronous callback registration and late completion

Selected additions: deep.md 39–45, Continuations And Callbacks. Reuse deep.md 8–38 and 49–51, standard.md 8–31, concurrency_route.md, quality.md and review.md already read; no reread needed. No implementation or illustrative example selected.

Required constraints:

- Checked continuation by default, but checked APIs alone do not establish a cancellation contract. Every success, failure and cancellation path competes for exactly one terminal result. Duplicate or late callbacks, cancellation before registration, during synchronous completion and after completion cannot resume twice, leak an unresolved continuation or publish obsolete state. Cancellation and completion require one synchronized linearization/terminal-state authority.
- Decide whether cancellation resumes/throws immediately, what underlying cancellation/unregistration does, and what happens if cancellation races a completed result. A continuation does not cancel the producer. Define cleanup and side-effect behavior separately from result publication.
- Initialize the state required by completion before invoking registration, because completion may execute synchronously inside that call. Registration may return its token after completion or cancellation already won; that late token still needs the required release/unregistration disposition. Cancellation before the token exists must not lose producer cleanup. No assumption that callback occurs only after registration returns is valid.
- Retain registration/subscription for exactly its required lifetime; unregister on cancellation or deinit as applicable, including completion cleanup required by the API. Owner release, synchronous callbacks and reentrant cancellation must not create retain cycles, dangling registration or conflicting cleanup. Do not assume unregister prevents an already-delivered callback. Do not hold a synchronization boundary across a foreign API call if synchronous/reentrant completion can deadlock; prove the actual synchronization model instead of stacking queues and actors.
- Preserve callback thread/isolation semantics explicitly. Foreign callbacks may arrive concurrently and outside the consumer actor. Terminal arbitration, mutable bridge state and actor publication each need ownership; moving to an actor must not create a second resumption path or change required timing silently. Transferred values must satisfy actual sendability/ownership constraints.

Unknowns: callback queue/executor and reentrancy; completion cardinality; registration token ownership and late-return behavior; unregister/cancel thread safety, blocking and idempotency; producer cancellation guarantee; terminal-result arbitration policy; lifetime/capture graph; value/error sendability and consumer isolation.
